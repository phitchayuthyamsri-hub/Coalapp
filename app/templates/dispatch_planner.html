Revised plan — one day, one cycle

This is where the readiness sheet takes effect again. The week was issued in advance and the subcontractor works to it; by the day itself the operation knows which trucks are actually there and when, so the day is rebuilt from the sheet — one cycle per truck, no loops issued forward. Everything else matches the week exactly: same figures, same planner, same bays shared across every company. Where this differs from the week, the day differed from the plan — not the arithmetic.

Against the issued plan

What the day costs, measured against the week as it was issued — not against a plan recomputed today, which would move with the day and always read "as planned". A minus is earlier than promised, a plus is later. The waits column is the answer to what did we miss: it names the stop that absorbed or created the difference.

The week, day by day

Each day is the trucks that reach the mine that day. The whole week is planned in one pass across every company, because the bays are shared — filtering to one company changes what you see, not who is in the queue ahead of it.

Every loop

One line per truck, running the whole week: every loop end to end, with a mark where one closes and the next begins. Colour is where the truck is — mine, border, QL49, port, the road home. Hover any stretch for what it is and the clock times either side of it. Where the same colour lines up across trucks, that is the corridor synchronising them, not a truck running late. A truck enters the cycle once, on its 1st dispatch from a readiness sheet; every loop after that is issued by the plan and is what the subcontractor works to. Later sheets do not move a truck that is already in the cycle. Only outside events do, through the revise pass.

Every timing

The same plan written out — one row per loop, every stop timed. This is what the Excel file contains, plus the waits broken down and the figures the plan was computed from.

The corridor

The loop every truck runs, drawn from the same polylines and geofences the planner calculates against. The strip beside it is the same loop read top to bottom — hover a leg there and it lights up on the map; click one to jump to the figure behind it.
